## v0.3.0 - 2026-11-18

v0.3.0 leaves go-socket a library of sockets alone, and makes `listen` remove only a socket it can show is its own, and give its mode only to its own. A program upgrading from v0.2 has these eight changes to make:

- `registry` and `wire` are gone, with no alias. Keep your own table of kinds, and serve `net/http` on the listener `listen.Listen` returns.
- `caller.Front`, `caller.Visitor`, `caller.FoldLogin` and `caller.SameLogin` are gone. Each was a program's own rule; keep your own.
- On Windows, `listen.Listen` serves a named pipe you name, `\\.\pipe\<name>`, in place of a Unix socket: give it that name, and dial it with `peer.DialPipe`. `peer.ListenSocket` is gone on every system: to let accounts in by SID, use `peer.ListenPipe`.
- On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, `listen.Listen` no longer replaces a socket left at the path unless you give `ReplaceEvenIfBusy`. Without it, such a socket is refused with `ErrMaybeBusy` and left.
- On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, a connection `listen`'s `Accept` returns is no longer a `*net.UnixConn`, and the socket's folder must take hard links. A type assertion to `*net.UnixConn` on an accepted connection fails; use the `net.Conn` methods, or `SyscallConn`, which it still has. A socket in a folder on FAT, exFAT or a share that takes no hard links now fails to listen: put it on a filesystem that takes them. So does a program whose AppArmor profile or SELinux policy lets it make only the socket and its lock file there: let it also make a folder there, link and remove names in it, and remove it.
- On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, `listen.SocketMode` must let the socket's owner write (0200), or `Listen` refuses it. Give 0600, or 0660 with `SocketGroup`.
- `caller.LoginProxy.RemoteUser` refuses a user or group with a space other than a space or tab at either end, where v0.2.0 took it off. Have your proxy write them without it.
- On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, a name another program put at the path since `listen.Listen` looked is refused with an error that wraps `ErrMaybeBusy` and `fs.ErrExist`, no longer `syscall.EADDRINUSE`. Check for `ErrMaybeBusy` in place of `syscall.EADDRINUSE`.

Each one is below, under Removed and Breaking, by package.

### Removed

**registry**
* The whole package: `New`, `Of` and its methods `Register`, `Open`, `Forget`, `Kinds` and `Here`, `Away`, `NotOnThisMachine` with its fields `Kind` and `Noun` and its methods `Error` and `Is`, `ErrNotHere` and `ErrUnknownKind`. It had nothing to do with sockets. A program that opens an implementation by its kind keeps its own table: a map from the kind to its open function, and an error of its own for a kind not on this machine.

**wire**
* The whole package: `WriteFrame`, `ReadFrame`, `ReadRaw`, `Parse`, `Decode`, `Envelope` with its fields `ID`, `Type`, `Body` and `Error`, `Error` with its fields `Kind`, `Msg` and `Hint` and its method `Error`, `ErrorOf`, `ErrOversized` with its fields `N` and `Max` and its method `Error`, `ErrNotAMessage`, `MaxMessage`, `KindRefused`, `KindBusy`, `KindFailed`, `Refused`, `Busy` and `Failed`. A program that spoke wire serves `net/http` on the listener `listen.Listen` returns. It caps each body itself with `http.MaxBytesReader`, and reads and writes its messages as JSON bodies, for a strict read with `encoding/json/v2` and `json.RejectUnknownMembers(true)`.

**caller**
* `Front`, with its fields `Who`, `May` and `Prefix` and its methods `Write` and `Read`, and `Visitor`, with its fields `Who` and `May`. A front of your own, one header for who is asking and one for what they may do, is your program's layout, so keep it in your program. If your front is a login proxy, read it with `LoginProxy`, giving the header names and separator your proxy writes.
* `FoldLogin` and `SameLogin`. When two logins are one person is your program's rule. `SameLogin(a, b)` was: `a` and `b` are equal once their ASCII capitals are made small, and nothing else is changed.

**peer**
* `ListenSocket`, on every system, with the Unix socket it made on Windows. To let accounts in by SID, use `peer.ListenPipe(name, sids...)` and dial with `peer.DialPipe`. For your account and SYSTEM alone, give `listen.Listen` the name `\\.\pipe\<name>`.

Nothing moved: every name above is gone, with no alias.

### Breaking

**listen**
* On Windows, `Listen` serves a named pipe, and you name it: `\\.\pipe\` and then a name, with no default. A path there, or a pipe's name on any other system, is refused with the new `ErrPipeName`. The pipe is owned by your account, and only your account and SYSTEM may open it, set as it is made. Clients on other machines are refused. A pipe already at the name, made by anyone, is `ErrHeld`. While your program's own ends of the pipe are open, the listener and each connection it accepted (which holds the name past the listener's `Close`), a `Listen` at the name is `ErrHeld`. Don't count on a client that keeps its end open to hold the name: on Windows 11 (build 26200) a restart's `Listen` took the name once the program's ends were closed, and that client's end reached nothing of the new pipe. A Windows that kept the name would answer that `Listen` with `ErrHeld` until the client closed. There is no lock file and no stale socket on Windows. `SocketMode`, `SocketGroup` and `ReplaceEvenIfBusy` are refused there with an error that wraps `errors.ErrUnsupported` (v0.2.0 refused `SocketMode`, whatever the mode, and `SocketGroup` there too; only `ReplaceEvenIfBusy`'s refusal is new). To upgrade a Windows program: give `Listen` `\\.\pipe\<name>` in place of a path, drop those three options, and dial with `peer.DialPipe` or go-winio's `DialPipe`. `peer.Listener` lets connections in by SID as it does on `peer.ListenPipe`.
* On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, `Listen` no longer replaces a socket at the path that refuses a connection (Windows has no socket there, above). A busy program that takes no lock refuses one the same way, and v0.2.0 could remove that program's live socket. Now `Listen` refuses it with the new `ErrMaybeBusy` and leaves it. A socket a crashed run left behind, which v0.2.0 replaced on its own, now needs the new option `ReplaceEvenIfBusy`. Give it only if your program alone listens at its path. On Linux, with the option, `Listen` first checks whether a socket is still bound to the file, and refuses one that is bound and not yet listening with `ErrHeld`.
* On Unix, `Listen` refuses a `SocketMode` that doesn't let its owner write (0200), with an error that wraps `fs.ErrInvalid`, before it makes anything: it dials its own socket to prove its path, and a connect needs write. Give a mode with 0200: 0600, or 0660 with `SocketGroup`.
* On Unix, a connection `Accept` returns is not a `*net.UnixConn`, though it has all of one's methods, `SyscallConn` among them, so a type assertion to `*net.UnixConn` fails. Use the `net.Conn` methods, or `SyscallConn`. Its `LocalAddr` is the path, and so is the listener's `Addr`.
* On Unix, a name another program put at the path since `Listen` looked is refused by the hard link (see Security), with an error that wraps `ErrMaybeBusy` and `fs.ErrExist`. It was the bind's `EADDRINUSE`, so a check for `syscall.EADDRINUSE` no longer matches: check `errors.Is(err, listen.ErrMaybeBusy)`.
* On Unix, the socket's folder must take hard links, so `Listen` fails on FAT, exFAT and some network shares. Put the socket on a filesystem that takes them.
* On Unix, `Listen` must be allowed to make a folder in the socket's folder, link and remove names in it, and remove it. A program whose AppArmor profile or SELinux policy grants only the socket and its lock file there now fails to listen, and nothing is left at the path. Grant those too.

**caller**
* `LoginProxy.RemoteUser` now trims only spaces and tabs from the user and each group, as HTTP trims a header value, where it trimmed every Unicode space. A user or group that still starts or ends with another space, a no-break space say, is now an error, where v0.2.0 read it with the space taken off. A vertical tab, form feed or line break at either end is an error too. Have your proxy write the user and groups without them.

### Changed

**listen**
* `Close` removes the socket only while `<path>.lock` is still the lock it holds and the path is still its socket. Otherwise it leaves the path and returns an error that wraps the new `ErrSocketLeft`.
* On every system, `Listen` refuses a group that isn't there before it makes anything.
* On Unix, the socket is bound in a private folder beside the path and hard linked to it (see Security). Besides the changes under Breaking, these things follow:
  * The socket's own address is still its private name, which is gone once the socket is at the path. A client's `RemoteAddr` and `getpeername(2)`, `ss -xl` and `/proc/net/unix` show that name, not the path.
  * A path is refused before anything is made if its private name doesn't fit a Unix socket's address. That name is the folder plus 10 bytes, so a short name in a folder whose own path is near that limit, which fit before, is now refused.
  * A private folder that can't be removed once the socket is at its path does not fail `Listen`. On an NFS client, for one, the private name stays in it as a `.nfsXXXX` name while the socket is open. `Close` removes the folder once the socket is closed, or says why it could not.
  * A process killed while `Listen` starts may leave a private folder beside the path, a `.` and six hex digits, and no later start removes it.

### Security

* **listen:** `Listen` no longer removes a live socket that another program bound at the path between `Listen`'s look and its bind. v0.2.0 bound through go-connections, which unlinks whatever is at the path first, so a program that takes no lock and listened there in that gap lost its socket, on every system. `Listen` now binds itself, and removes only a socket `ReplaceEvenIfBusy` lets it replace. On Unix it then proves the socket at the path is its own: it looks at the path, dials it with a fresh nonce, accepts that dial on its own socket, and looks again. A path another program unlinked or bound since is left, and `Listen` fails with `ErrMaybeBusy`, where v0.2.0's `Close` would have removed that program's socket. A client connection made before the proof is handed, unread, to the first `Accept`. When the proof's dial fails, a ctx that ended among the ways, `Listen` removes the path while it is still its own socket's file, so a `Listen` that fails for its own reason leaves no socket at the path. It fails with the dial's own error, which wraps the ctx's, and not with `ErrMaybeBusy`; another program's socket there is left, and that failure wraps `ErrMaybeBusy`. A failure before the socket is at its path removes nothing there. One gap remains, in the two removals without `ReplaceEvenIfBusy`: `Close` and a failed proof each look at the path and then remove it, and a program that takes no lock and swaps the path in the instant between the two still loses its socket. `Listen`'s doc names it.
* **listen:** On macOS, Linux and the BSDs, `Listen` no longer gives its socket's mode and group to another program's socket. v0.2.0 set them by path once bound, so a program that takes no lock, and unlinked and bound at the path in that instant, had its socket given them. With `SocketMode(0o666)` or `SocketGroup`, that opened its socket to others. `Listen` now binds in a private folder beside the path, made 0700, and gives the socket its mode and group there. It listens, and only then puts the socket at the path with a hard link, which refuses a name already there and leaves it. The private name and folder are removed on every way out, or by `Close` where the folder can't be removed while the socket is open.
